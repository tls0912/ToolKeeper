# The story of 汗青

The name “汗青” (Hanqing) comes from the ancient method of preparing bamboo slips.

## From bamboo slips to the annals of history

Before paper was widely used, people recorded words on bamboo slips. Fresh bamboo contains moisture and is vulnerable to damp and insect damage, so it first had to be heated over a fire. Moisture seeped out of the bamboo as though it were sweating, and this process was called “汗青”. Once treated, the slips could be preserved for a long time; the words written on them could travel across the years and reach later generations.

Over time, “汗青” came to mean more than a step in preparing bamboo slips. It became a name for historical records and history itself.

Wen Tianxiang of the Southern Song dynasty wrote in *Crossing the Lingding Ocean*:

> 「人生自古誰無死，留取丹心照汗青。」
>
> Who since ancient times has lived and never died? Let my loyal heart shine in the annals of history.
>
> — Wen Tianxiang, *Crossing the Lingding Ocean*

Here, “汗青” means the annals of history.

## For thoughts worth keeping

Our lives are finite, and memories gradually fade, but words can remain.

An article, a note, or a thought that suddenly comes to mind may seem like a few ordinary lines at the time. Yet years later, those lines may bring back the thoughts and memories of that moment.

From bamboo slips and paper to today's digital documents, the tools of writing keep changing, but our reasons for leaving words behind remain the same.

**We record because we do not want to forget.**

**We write because some thoughts deserve to be kept.**

## Let words take the centre

This is why “汗青” came into being.

It is a tool for reading and writing Markdown, and a place where words can quietly rest.

We do not want a complicated interface to interrupt our thoughts, or the tool itself to overshadow the content. Open a document, read, write, save. That is all. **Let the tool step aside, and let words take the centre.**

## The meaning of writing things down

People once wrote their words on bamboo slips; today, we leave ours on screens.

The medium has changed. The meaning has not.

Not every passage needs to become a celebrated work, and not every note needs to be seen by the world. Some words belong only to ourselves; some records exist simply to remember what we thought on a particular day.

But once those words are written down, that moment no longer disappears completely.

---

**The bamboo slips are long gone. The words remain.**

**This is “汗青”.**
